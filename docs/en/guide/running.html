    Switching to running mode

    Tap the runner side of the two-way button at the right of the Home header, and the whole app switches to running mode. You can also switch from the Training mode row in Menu.

    1. In the dumbbell and runner button at the right of the Home header, tap Running.
    2. To go back, tap Strength on the same button.
    • Home: today's run card, this week's distance with bars for the last 8 weeks, shortcuts to My courses, Pace zones, and Weekly plan, and recent runs.
    • Record: opens on Runs only, and you can switch to All workouts.
    • Stats: a running section appears at the top, with the strength sections unchanged below it.
    • Coach: the running-focused weekly plan sits at the top (PRO+).
    • Menu: rows for My courses, Pace zones, and Run settings appear.

    The mode only changes the screens. Your log, prescriptions, and health app data stay in one place with your strength training. On days with strength scheduled, Home shows Today is a strength day · Switch to strength. If a workout is in progress, you'll see You have a workout in progress. Switch after you finish. and the mode won't change. The mode you pick stays after you reopen the app.

    Starting a run

    In running mode, tap Start run on today's run card on Home, then pick the kind of run and the device that measures it.

    1. On Home, tap Start run on today's run card.
    2. The card at the top is today's prescription (or a suggestion if there isn't one). Tap Start run on it, or pick a kind below such as Free run, Time trial, Course run, Track, or Treadmill.
    3. Under Measure with, pick Phone or Watch.
    4. The first time you measure outdoors with your phone, it asks for location. Allow location while using the app.

    Phone measures with your phone's satellite location and keeps going with the screen off until that run ends. Treadmill estimates distance from your steps without location. Time trial is a hard effort that sets your pace zones. Structured workout (step-by-step threshold, interval, and hill sessions) and Race (goal pace and finish prediction) are PRO. To measure with a watch, see Running with a watch.

    The running screen and voice guidance

    While you run, distance and current pace are shown large, with tiles below for grade-adjusted pace, heart rate, cadence, lap, ascent, and average pace.

    • The top shows what's measuring (such as Measuring on phone) and location status (such as Finding GPS).
    • In steps with a target, the Target bar shows In range, Too fast, or Too slow.
    • Tap Lap to split a lap yourself, and use Pause, Resume, and Finish. In structured workouts, Next step moves to the next step.
    • On a course, you see the distance left and whether you're ahead of or behind your past self, and Off course if you leave it.

    Voice guidance announces laps, steps, leaving your range, and going off course through your earphones. It plays from your phone when the phone measures; when a watch measures, the watch vibrates instead. Change Voice guidance, Auto pause, Auto lap, and Current pace window in Menu → Run settings. With Auto pause on, time spent standing still, like at a traffic light, isn't counted.

    Results and the evidence behind each metric

    When you finish, you see a map of your route, distance, time, average pace, ascent, Splits, and Analysis. Tap a metric to see its formula, evidence, and limits.

    • Splits: pace, heart rate, and grade for each split. Grade-adjusted values per split are PRO.
    • Analysis PRO: Grade-adjusted pace, Heart-rate drift, Heart-rate load, Session load, cadence, and terrain.
    • How hard was it?: pick 1 to 10, and it goes into session load and fitness and fatigue.
    • Save as course saves this route as a course, and Export file sends a GPX file to another app.

    Grade-adjusted pace estimates what your pace would have been on flat ground, and it tends to overstate the benefit of downhills. Heart-rate drift compares efficiency between the first and second halves, and is only calculated for steady runs longer than 20 minutes. If this run makes a good reference result, a Use as reference button appears. Open past runs again from Record.

    Reference result and pace zones

    Enter one recent result as your reference, and your Easy, Marathon, Threshold, and Interval paces and predicted times are set.

    1. Open Menu → Pace zones. You can also use the Pace zones shortcut on the running-mode Home.
    2. Tap Add result, pick 3 km, 5 km, 10 km, Half marathon, or Marathon, and enter Time (min:sec).
    3. Check Predicted times and Heart-rate zone basis (Max heart rate or Threshold heart rate).

    Paces come from the Daniels and Gilbert oxygen cost formula (VDOT), and How these paces are calculated shows the formula and sources. Without a result, run a Time trial or tap Use as reference on a run result. After 8 weeks, the app suggests measuring again.

    Prescriptions that know your strength training

    A single run is kept within 110% of your longest run in the last 30 days. With the weekly running plan, a quality run the day after a leg workout becomes an easy run.

    • Kept within 110% of your longest run in 30 days: in an observational study of 5,200 runners over 18 months, runs that went past this line had a higher injury risk. If you lengthen a run yourself, the app only tells you and doesn't stop you. Your current limit is under Single-run limit in Stats.
    • Made easier after yesterday's leg workout PRO+: this follows a randomized trial in which 30-minute run times were about 4% slower two days after muscle damage. A one-line reason appears on today's card only on days it changes.
    • No recent reference result, so a time trial is suggested: without a reference result, or with an old one, the plan's first quality day becomes a time trial.

    Make the weekly running plan from Plan a running-focused week at the top of Coach in running mode (PRO+). It spreads easy and quality runs so they don't clash with strength days, and moves only the runs, leaving your strength prescriptions as they are.

    Making and importing courses

    Run the same route twice and it becomes a course on its own. You can also make one in Menu → My courses from a past run, by drawing on the map, or from a file.

    1. Open Menu → My courses and tap Create course.
    2. Pick Past run, Draw on map, or File.
    3. Name it and save, then run it with Run this course on the course screen.

    Draw on map joins the points you tap on the map with straight lines. In builds where maps aren't available, this option is hidden. Files can be GPX or TCX. When you run a course, you race your past self, and My best and your runs build up on the course screen. Marking part of a course as My segments to see records for just that stretch is PRO.

    Sending and receiving courses

    From Send course on the course screen, send it as a Link, QR code, or File. Nothing goes through a server.

    1. In Menu → My courses, open the course to send.
    2. In Send course, pick how to send it, and turn on Include my record if you want.
    3. The other person taps the link or scans the QR code, then saves it in the You received a course window.

    Opened in a phone browser, the link shows the course drawing and distance with a button to open it in the app. You can also open a copied link with Open copied link under ⋯ at the top right of My courses. When you send, the start and finish areas are hidden at random, and times and heart rate aren't included. An included record carries only the time for each stretch, with no date or clock time. For long courses you'll see This course is too long for a link. Send it as a file., so send a file.

    Hiding your location

    When you send a course or export a file, a random length is cut from around the start and finish. Set areas around home or work as Privacy zones.

    1. Open Menu → Run settings.
    2. Under Hide start and finish when sharing, pick a distance to hide. A random 0–200 m is added to it before cutting.
    3. Under Privacy zones, tap Hide around a recent start to hide the area around where your most recent run started.

    The center of each hidden circle is moved a little from the real spot and fixed once, so averaging many shared runs doesn't reveal the place. Result maps and records on your own device aren't hidden and stay as they are.

    Running with a watch

    You can measure with Apple Watch, Galaxy Watch, or a Garmin watch. Pick Watch on the start screen, then start on the watch.

    1. Connect your watch first.
    2. On the start screen, under Measure with, pick Watch and tap Start run.
    3. When your phone shows Start on your watch, start in FitBoost on the watch.

    The watch shows pace ranges, steps, and the course line, and vibrates when you leave the range. Apple Watch also vibrates 5 seconds before the next step. When you finish, the run comes to your phone. Choose satellite settings for Garmin watches in Menu → Run settings → Satellites (Garmin); Max accuracy uses more battery. Watches have no separate mode and show the run you picked on your phone.

    Free and paid in running

    Measuring is all free. PRO adds analysis that interprets your runs and structured workouts, and PRO+ adds a weekly running plan that works around strength recovery.

    • Free: measuring with your phone or three kinds of watch, live pace and heart rate, auto laps and splits, reference result, pace zones, and predicted times, my courses (same-route detection, drawing, files, receiving), sending by link, QR code, or file, GPX export, privacy zones, writing to health apps, the single-run limit, and metric evidence.
    • PRO: grade-adjusted pace, heart-rate drift, heart-rate load, session load, critical speed, fitness and fatigue combined with strength, structured workouts (step-by-step threshold, interval, and hill sessions), race-day mode (goal pacer and finish prediction), and my segment records on courses.
    • PRO+: a running-focused weekly plan that works around strength recovery (including easing off the day after a leg workout).

    Locked tiles show a lock, and tapping one opens the FitBoost PRO screen. If your subscription ends, analysis values for runs you've already recorded aren't deleted, only hidden. There are no ads on the running screen.
