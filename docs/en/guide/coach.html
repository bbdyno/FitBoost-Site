Plan, coaching and races

How the Plan tab is laid out, the strength coach and next-weight suggestions, coach circuits, weekly plans, block programs, and choosing, practicing and analyzing races in the Race tab.

    The Plan tab and the coach screen

    Plan has what to do each day this week and the What goes into your week pills. The Coach tab is gone in 2.0.0; open the coach screen with the Strength coach and Running coach pills.

    • The strip at the top: if you've set a race date, it draws the twelve weeks up to the race as Base, Build, Peak, and Taper phases. With no race prep and a weekly plan, it shows a bar in the sport's color for each day of this week.
    • This week PRO+: what to do each day from Monday to Sunday (coach session, routine, circuit or run) and which days are done or missed; a day with nothing planned is Rest day. Tap a row to open the plan screen. Without PRO+ it's the locked Plan your week row. See Weekly plan.
    • Running alongside lifting: appears when you've done both strength and running recently. See Prescriptions that know your strength training.
    • The Race prep pill: takes you to Race. See Race prep and practice.
    • Strength pills: Strength coach (block programs, deloads and the readiness check; only the movement assessment row is free and the rest is PRO+), Programs, My routines, Movement assessment, and Coach circuit.
    • Running pills: Running coach (the running coach, free), Form analysis, and Pace zones.
    • The Body & nutrition section of the coach screen: cardio fitness (free), plus nutrition targets, body composition coaching and stress and recovery (PRO+). Open it from the section picker at the top of the coach screen.

    The order of the strength and running pills follows your main focus. The coach screen opens on the section of the pill you tapped, and the section picker at the top always sits in the same order: Strength, Running, Body & nutrition.

    Next-weight suggestions during a workout PRO

    Finish a set and log your effort, and the app tells you what to lift on the next set and when to stop for the day.

    Turn suggestions on or off in Me → App settings → Workout & routines → In-workout coaching. Free users see a one-time preview per workout. PRO+ also adjusts back-off sets on the spot from your top-set effort and fills in warm-up sets for you.

    Suggestions are a training aid, not medical advice. If something hurts, go lighter and log it with This hurts.

    Coach circuits

    Pick your time, equipment, and intensity, and the app builds a circuit for today. Free gets 3 a week; PRO has no limit.

    1. Tap Circuit in the shortcuts on Today. You can also open it from the Coach circuit pill on Plan, or from ⋯ → Get a circuit from your coach next to Start on Today.
    2. The first time, choose time, equipment, and intensity and tap Build circuit. After that, the preview opens with your last choices.
    3. In the preview, use Rebuild, Save as routine, or Circuit settings (voice guidance and sounds), then tap Start.

    When you finish, it asks how it felt and adjusts the next circuit. You can also start one from your watch.

    Weekly plan PRO+

    It builds a week that mixes strength, circuits, and running. There's one plan, and when you change it, today's workout on Today and the This week list on Plan update right away.

    1. On Plan, tap the Plan your week row. If you already have a plan, tap a row in the This week list. You can also open it from Plan → Programs → Weekly plan, or from the Weekly plan shortcut in the Running group on the Today → This week screen.
    2. Choose days, goal, focus, cardio, equipment, and age, then tap Build plan. The focus starts from your main focus.
    3. Today's workout shows at the top of Today. On the plan screen, start a day with its Start button.

    To change the settings, tap Rebuild plan. The plan's week always runs Monday to Sunday, whatever first day of the week you set in settings. Routines you edited yourself or put on your schedule are kept, not overwritten. A missed day stays missed and isn't moved to another day. The week after you miss a strength or circuit day, the plan's routines repeat last time's weight and reps instead of going up. If you have a block program, its coach sessions go on the plan's strength days in order, and if you're doing a cardio fitness block, its sessions go on the cardio days.

    Choosing Running focus as the focus gives a plan with more running days, and swaps a quality run for an easy run the day after a leg workout. See the running coach and Prescriptions that know your strength training.

    Block programs in the strength coach PRO+

    From your goal, meet date, and current 1RMs, it builds a multi-week program and sets the weights for every session.

    1. Tap Plan → Strength coach → Build program.
    2. Choose your goal (powerlifting, strength, powerbuilding, or hypertrophy), days per week, experience, progression style, body weight, and meet date (optional).
    3. Enter your current 1RMs. If you don't know them, calculate from a weight and reps.
    4. Tap Build program. Today's session appears on the coach screen and on Today.

    Block programs in the Strength section are PRO+ only. Without PRO+ you'll see the movement assessment row, an introduction, and See PRO+. If you have a weekly plan, make one from the Run strength days on a coach program row and its coach sessions go on the plan's strength days in order.

    Readiness check PRO+

    Log sleep, muscle soreness, stress, and energy before training, and it adjusts that day's weights and sets.

    Open the coach screen with Strength coach or Running coach on Plan, tap Check in on the Readiness card, fill it in, and tap Save and apply. With a program running, you can also open it from Readiness on Today. Connect a health app or Garmin Connect to include sleep duration, HRV, and resting heart rate.

    Deloads and meet attempts PRO+

    It reads your performance and fatigue trends and suggests a deload or a block extension on the strength coach screen.

    It recommends a deload when recent performance drops, sessions feel harder than prescribed, readiness stays low, or six weeks have passed since your last deload. When the program reaches the weeks right before a meet (peaking and taper), it also shows first, second, and third attempts.

    Nutrition targets PRO+

    It reads your body composition or body-weight trend and resets your calorie, carb, protein, and fat targets every week.

    On the Nutrition card in the Body & nutrition section of the coach screen you open from Plan, enter weight, height, age, activity, and goal, then tap Set targets. With body composition (lean mass) from the last 30 days, maintenance calories and protein come from lean mass instead of body weight, and entering Target body fat shows a goal weight (see Using body composition). The weight trend appears after at least 4 weigh-ins over more than 2 weeks. For a one-off calculation, use the macro calculator.

    Race prep and practice

    On Race, practice the race you're preparing for as if it were the real thing, timing every segment and keeping the record. Practice is free up to 3 times a week.

    1. Open Race. The race you're preparing for is at the top; to switch to another one, tap Change race (see Choosing a race and making your own).
    2. Tap Race practice to open the prep screen. Race practice in the shortcuts on Today opens the same screen.
    3. Pick Division (weights), Practice scope, and Goal finish time (full race), and each segment's distance or reps, weight, and target appear on a segment line. To time the move to each station separately, turn on Time transitions separately. For doubles and relay, see Team plan.
    4. Tap Start race practice and press the big button at the bottom each time you finish a segment. You can also finish it on your watch.
    5. When you're done, you'll see the split table and the segments where you lost the most time. Save with Save and finish.

    During practice, the time on the current segment is shown largest, and below it are the time left to your target and a segment line showing the finished, current, and next segments. On a segment scored by weight or distance, the time limit counts down, and when you move on the app asks for the weight you lifted or the distance you covered. Tap Enter later and you can fill it in afterwards with Enter in the split table. When the time limit runs out, the app only tells you and doesn't move on by itself.

    Pick a goal from the preset times, or enter hours, minutes and seconds with Custom (5 minutes to 5 hours). Your goal resets the segment targets and the run pace, station total and transition time. If the goal is more than 2% faster than your fastest full practice or the estimate from your week-one diagnosis, the app warns it may be too much. Segment estimates follow studies measured at real races and coaching methods, and parts with no research are marked as field estimates. See the sources under How it is calculated. For a race with no finisher distribution data, the app doesn't split your goal into segments or make up an estimate; it compares against your own past practices. A race that isn't decided on time alone has no goal finish time.

    Pick a race date with Pick race date on the prep screen and the remaining weeks are split into Base, Build, Peak, and Taper with what to do now, and Race and Plan show the days left to the race and your current phase. For a race with distribution data, a week-one diagnosis also finds your weakest station. This is a practice tool not tied to any particular event; distances, reps and weights follow the public rules, and values that couldn't be confirmed from official sources are shown as Unconfirmed.

    A practice started on your phone can be tracked on Apple Watch, Galaxy Watch or a Garmin watch, showing the current segment, its distance or reps and target, the time on that segment, and wrist heart rate. To end a segment, tap Lap on Apple Watch and Galaxy Watch, or press the start button on Garmin. Run segment distance is measured with your phone's location. A segment where you enter a weight or distance ends without a value if you move on from the watch, so fill it in from the split table on your phone.

    Choosing a race and making your own

    Tap Change race on Race to open the Choose a race screen, where you pick one of eleven hybrid race formats or build your own race with Make your own.

    1. Tap Change race next to the race name at the top of Race.
    2. Each format shows a generic name, the number of segments, a small strip that draws the segment order, and whether it's confirmed (Confirmed or Partly unconfirmed). Tap one to see each division's segments on a segment line.
    3. Tap Prepare for this race and that race becomes My race; practice, adding results, and the race practice days in your weekly plan all follow its segment order.

    In Make your own, enter a Race name, lay out the segments in order with Add a run and Add a station, then tap Save. For a station, enter its name, amount (meters, reps, or calories), and weight. Races with no running, races with different run distances, and races scored by weight or distance are practiced on the same screen. The Running race row on the Choose a race screen and at the bottom of Race is for running races such as a marathon or half marathon, and opens the run start screen with Race already picked (PRO). Race names are trademarks of their organizers, and FitBoost is an independent app that isn't affiliated with or sponsored by any race.

    Adding a race result and its analysis

    Enter your race finish time and splits with Add result on Race, and Race analysis shows where you lost time.

    1. On Race, tap Add result.
    2. Enter your Finish time, then as many Splits as you know, in the segment order of the race you chose. You can leave blank the ones you don't know.
    3. Tap Save and analyze.

    The analysis runs in this order: a one-line verdict, your finish time, the segment strip, the segments where you lost the most time, Split by split, and your next goal. For a race with finisher distribution data, it shows your percentile against finishers in the same division, and Set as goal makes the next goal your race goal. For a race with no distribution data, it doesn't make up a percentile or a next goal; it compares against your own past results in the same race. With no splits, it works from the finish time alone. For races the results site covers, paste your result link under Read from link and the app reads just that one result. Results you add and past practices collect under Past races and practice on Race; tap one to open it again.

    Doubles and relay divisions and the team plan

    Pick a Doubles or Relay (4) division and the key rules appear, and the Team plan card uses your teammates' diagnoses to decide who does what and how much.

    1. On the prep screen you open with Race practice on Race, tap Doubles or Relay (4) and pick a division (women, men, mixed and so on).
    2. On the Team plan card, tap a teammate and fill in Teammate tests (1 km run, row, farmers carry, wall balls). Enter your own diagnosis on the race prep card above after setting a race date.
    3. See the team estimate, each station's split, and the pace to run together or the legs each person takes.
    • Doubles: you run every 1 km together, at the pace of whoever is slower alone. Station shares are set so both work for the same time, which means the faster person does more, with switch units such as 250 m on the ergs or 12.5 m on the sled. Mixed doubles both use the men's open weights.
    • Relay: each of the four takes a 1 km run and the station after it twice, and legs are assigned to each person's strong stations to give the lowest total. Mixed relay needs two women and two men, each using their own sex's weights.

    Teammates with no diagnosis are calculated from typical results. In doubles practice, each segment name carries both people's shares; in relay, the runner's name. These go to your watch as well. Switch units with no research behind them (such as 10 wall balls) are marked as field estimates.
