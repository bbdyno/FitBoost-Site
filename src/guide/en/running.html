Where running lives

From 1.9.1 there's no running mode; running is always on the same screens as strength. Find it in the {{> sectionRunning}} group on {{> tabHome}} and the {{> sectionRunning}} sections of {{> tabStats}} and {{> tabCoach}}.

  1. If the top card on {{> tabHome}} isn't today's run, tap {{> runStart}} right below it. The run start screen opens without asking anything.
  2. To show running first, set {{> trainingMixTitle}} to {{> trainingMixRunning}}.
  • {{> tabHome}}: the {{> sectionRunning}} group has the {{> courseShelfTitle}} row, this week's distance with bars for the last 8 weeks, shortcuts to {{> courseTitle}}, {{> runZonesTitle}}, and {{> runHomeHybridPlan}}, {{> assessEntryRunTitle}}, recent runs, and {{> runImportFile}}.
  • {{> tabRecord}}: the {{> historyRunsOnly}} chip shows only runs.
  • {{> tabStats}}: running stats are in the {{> sectionRunning}} section.
  • {{> tabCoach}}: the {{> sectionRunning}} section is the running coach, and strength coaching such as block plans is in the {{> sectionStrength}} section.
  • {{> tabMenu}}: the {{> sectionRunning}} group has {{> courseTitle}}, {{> runZonesTitle}}, {{> vo2Title}}, {{> runSettingsTitle}}, and {{> assessEntryRunTitle}}.

Your log, prescriptions and health app data are shared with strength training. If a strength workout is in progress, a run won't start and the app tells you why. If you try to add an exercise during a run, you'll see {{" exerciseBlockedDuringRun}}

Starting a run

Tap today's run card at the top of {{> tabHome}}, or {{> runStart}} right below the top card, then pick the kind of run and the device that measures it.

  1. If the top card on {{> tabHome}} is today's run, tap {{> runStart}} on it; if it's a strength card, tap {{> runStart}} right below it.
  2. The card at the top is today's prescription (or a suggestion if there isn't one). Tap {{> runStart}} on it, or pick a kind below such as {{> runTitleFree}}, {{> runKindTest}}, {{> runTitleCourse}}, {{> runTitleTrack}}, or {{> runKindTreadmill}}.
  3. Under {{> runDeviceTitle}}, pick {{> runDevicePhone}} or {{> runDeviceWatch}}.
  4. The first time you measure outdoors with your phone, it asks for location. Allow location while using the app.

{{> runDevicePhone}} measures with your phone's satellite location and keeps going with the screen off until that run ends. {{> runKindTreadmill}} estimates distance from your steps without location. {{> runKindTest}} is a hard effort that sets your pace zones. {{> runKindStructured}} (step-by-step threshold, interval, and hill sessions) and {{> runTitleRace}} (goal pace and finish prediction) are PRO. To measure with a watch, see Running with a watch.

The running screen and voice guidance

While you run, distance and current pace are shown large, with tiles below for grade-adjusted pace, heart rate, cadence, lap, ascent, and average pace.

  • The top shows what's measuring (such as {{> runSourcePhone}}) and location status (such as {{> runGpsSearching}}).
  • In steps with a target, the {{> runTarget}} bar shows {{> runInRange}}, {{> runTooFast}}, or {{> runTooSlow}}.
  • Tap {{> runLap}} to split a lap yourself, and use {{> runPause}}, {{> runResume}}, and {{> runFinish}}. In structured workouts, {{> runNextStepButton}} moves to the next step.
  • On a course, you see the distance left and whether you're ahead of or behind your past self, and {{" runOffCourse}} if you leave it.

Voice guidance announces laps, steps, leaving your range, and going off course through your earphones. It plays from your phone when the phone measures; when a watch measures, the watch vibrates instead. Change {{> runSettingsVoice}}, {{> runSettingsAutoPause}}, {{> runSettingsLap}}, and {{> runSettingsPaceWindow}} in {{> tabMenu > runSettingsTitle}}. With {{> runSettingsAutoPause}} on, time spent standing still, like at a traffic light, isn't counted.

Results and the evidence behind each metric

When you finish, you see a map of your route, distance, time, average pace, ascent, {{> runSplits}}, and {{> runAnalysis}}. Tap a metric to see its formula, evidence, and limits.

  • {{> runSplits}}: pace, heart rate, and grade for each split. Grade-adjusted values per split are PRO.
  • {{> runAnalysis}} PRO: {{> runGap}}, {{> runDecoupling}}, {{> runTrimp}}, {{> runSessionLoad}}, cadence, and terrain.
  • {{> runRpeTitle}}: pick 1 to 10, and it goes into session load and fitness and fatigue.
  • {{> runSaveCourse}} in the card under the map saves this route as a course, and {{> courseSendToFriend}} in the same card sends it right away. On a course you know, you see how you did against your best. {{> runExport}} sends a GPX file to another app.

Grade-adjusted pace estimates what your pace would have been on flat ground, and it tends to overstate the benefit of downhills. Heart-rate drift compares efficiency between the first and second halves, and is only calculated for steady runs longer than 20 minutes. If this run makes a good reference result, a {{> runUseBaseline}} button appears. Open past runs again from {{> tabRecord}}.

Tap {{> shareDecorate}} right under the summary to make or decorate a route, splits, photo, or sticker card and post it straight to your Instagram story. See Choosing a share card.

Importing runs from other apps

Pick a GPX or TCX activity file exported from another app or watch under {{> runImportFile}} at the bottom of the {{> sectionRunning}} group on {{> tabHome}}, and it becomes a run in your log.

  1. Export the run as a GPX or TCX file from the other app or your watch app, and keep it on your phone.
  2. Tap {{> runImportFile}} at the bottom of the {{> sectionRunning}} group on {{> tabHome}} and pick the file. If you have no runs yet, it's also in the {{> sectionRunning}} section of {{> tabStats}}.
  3. When the result screen opens, share and customize it with {{> shareDecorate}} just like a run you tracked in the app.

Only activity files with timestamps come in as runs, and heart rate and cadence are read too when the file has them. If you pick a course file without timestamps, you'll see {{" runImportFailed}} Open course files in Making and importing courses. If you import a run that started at the same time again, no new one is made and the existing run opens. Runs that other apps wrote to your health app come in automatically when you open the app, without a file; see Runs from your health app.

Reference result and pace zones

Enter one recent result as your reference, and your {{> runZoneEasy}}, {{> runZoneMarathon}}, {{> runZoneThreshold}}, and {{> runZoneInterval}} paces and predicted times are set.

  1. Open {{> tabMenu > runZonesTitle}}. You can also use the {{> runZonesTitle}} shortcut in the {{> sectionRunning}} group on {{> tabHome}}.
  2. Tap {{> runBaselineSet}}, pick 3 km, 5 km, 10 km, {{> runHalf}}, or {{> runFull}}, and enter {{> runBaselineTime}}.
  3. Check {{> runPredictions}} and {{> runHrBasis}} ({{> runHrBasisMax}} or {{> runHrBasisThreshold}}).

Paces come from the Daniels and Gilbert oxygen cost formula (VDOT), and {{> runZonesHow}} shows the formula and sources. Without a result, run a {{> runKindTest}} or tap {{> runUseBaseline}} on a run result. After 8 weeks, the app suggests measuring again.

Running coach

Pick the {{> sectionRunning}} section below the shared area of {{> tabCoach}} to get the running coach. The top card shows what to run today and why, and you can tap {{> runStart}} right there. It's free.

  1. Open {{> tabCoach}} and tap {{> sectionRunning}} in the section picker. Next time, the last section you viewed opens.
  2. In the top card, check {{> runTodayPlan}} (that day's prescription from your hybrid plan) or {{> runSuggested}} (the run we suggest when there's no plan).
  3. Read the reasons on the card and tap {{> runStart}}.
  • Reasons: a line is added when the run was eased for leg recovery, kept within 110% of your longest run in the last 30 days, turned into a time trial because there's no reference result, or trimmed to match your recent runs.
  • {{> runCoachLegsTitle}}: without lower-body strength records, recovery isn't taken into account. If lower-body recovery is below 70% or you trained legs hard yesterday, it tells you to swap a quality run for an easy run.
  • {{> runZonesTitle}} and {{> runCapTitle}}: your pace zones, plus this week's distance and the single-run limit.
  • {{> assessEntryRunTitle}}: the running form tests and your cadence target.
  • Readiness check PRO+: with PRO+, this section also has the readiness card.

The weekly plan, {{> coachInterferenceTitle}} and hybrid race prep sit in the shared area above the sections, so you see them in every section. Strength coaching such as block plans and volume is in the {{> sectionStrength}} section, and cardio fitness training and nutrition are in the {{> sectionBodyNutrition}} section. The first time you open the {{> sectionRunning}} section, an intro tip shows once. On iPad and an unfolded iPhone Duo, when the Coach tab opens on the {{> sectionRunning}} section, the coach item in the left menu shows {{> largeRunCoach}}, and on a run day the recovery item shows leg recovery.

Prescriptions that know your strength training

A single run is kept within 110% of your longest run in the last 30 days. With the weekly running plan, a quality run the day after a leg workout becomes an easy run.

  • {{" runReasonCapped}}: in an observational study of 5,200 runners over 18 months, runs that went past this line had a higher injury risk. If you lengthen a run yourself, the app only tells you and doesn't stop you. Your current limit is under {{> runCapTitle}} in {{> tabStats}}.
  • {{" runReasonLegs}} PRO+: this follows a randomized trial in which 30-minute run times were about 4% slower two days after muscle damage. A one-line reason appears on today's card only on days it changes.
  • {{" runReasonBaselineTest}}: without a reference result, or with an old one, the plan's first quality day becomes a time trial.
  • The {{" runLegsCheckTitle}} window: on a day your leg recovery is below 70% or you trained legs hard yesterday, picking a quality run (threshold, interval, or hills; structured sessions are PRO) without a plan shows this before you start. Choose {{> runLegsEasy}}, {{> runLegsSlower}} (laps and repeats stay the same), or {{> runLegsAsPlanned}}. This follows a randomized study in which 30-minute times were about 4% slower 48 hours after muscle damage (Marcora 2007).
  • Running after lifting: open the run start screen within 6 hours of finishing a strength workout, and a spacing note appears at the top. Cardio right after lifting gave the smallest strength gains, and the researchers advised avoiding gaps under 6 hours (Robineau 2016).
  • Legs after running: open {{> tabHome}} within 8 hours of a run with lower body in today's strength routine, and today's card gets a line saying lower-body reps may drop, so keep the weight. Weight and sets don't change. This follows a study in which leg press reps fell 4 and 8 hours after cardio, with no difference after 24 hours and no effect on the upper body (Sporer and Wenger 2003).
  • {{> coachInterferenceTitle}}: when you have both strength training in the last 30 days and runs in the last 7 days, this row appears in the shared area at the top of {{> tabCoach}}. It shows your running days and time in the last 7 days, along with a meta-analysis in which strength and muscle gains shrank as endurance frequency and duration rose (Wilson 2012). If you have a hybrid plan this week, it also shows that plan's strength, circuit and cardio counts. There's no set threshold.

For a week with lots of running, make a weekly hybrid plan and set {{> hybridFocus}} to {{> hybridFocusRunning}} (PRO+). It spreads easy and quality runs so they don't clash with strength days, and swaps a quality run for an easy run the day after a leg workout.

Making and importing courses

Your courses sit in the {{> courseShelfTitle}} row of the {{> sectionRunning}} group on {{> tabHome}}, nearest to where you last ran first. Save one in a tap from a run result, or make one from a past run, by drawing on the map, or from a file.

  1. On the result screen after a run, tap {{> runSaveCourse}} under the map and it becomes a course right away. It takes the run's name, which you can change on the course screen.
  2. Otherwise, use the card at the end of the {{> courseShelfTitle}} row or {{> courseMake}} in {{> tabMenu > courseTitle}}, then pick {{> courseFromRun}}, {{> courseDraw}}, or {{> courseFromFile}}.
  3. Tap {{> courseRunThis}} at the top of the course screen to open {{> runTitleCourse}}. Choose {{> runDevicePhone}} or {{> runDeviceWatch}} under {{> runDeviceTitle}} and run.

Running the same route twice also makes a course on its own. In {{> courseTitle}}, filter with {{> courseFilterAll}}, {{> courseOriginReceived}}, {{> courseLoop}}, and distance chips. Nearest-first uses where your latest run started, without asking for location permission. The {{> courseVersus}} card on the course screen lists {{> courseBestLabel}}, {{> courseVersusReceived}}, and {{> courseVersusLatest}} times fastest first, and running the course races you against them. With a watch, the course line shows on the watch and it vibrates when you go off course. {{> courseDraw}} joins the points you tap on the map with straight lines. Pan and zoom the map and tap points along the road. Files can be GPX or TCX. To bring in a whole run you recorded in another app, use Importing runs from other apps. Marking part of a course as {{> courseSegments}} to see records for just that stretch is PRO.

Sending and receiving courses

Tap {{> courseSendToFriend}} at the top of the course screen to open {{> courseSendTitle}}. You see the shape that will be sent first, then send it with {{> courseShareLinkSend}}, {{> courseShareQr}}, or {{> courseShareGpx}}. Nothing goes through a server.

  1. Open the course and tap {{> courseSendToFriend}}. A course you just saved on a run result can be sent right there with {{> courseSendToFriend}}.
  2. Check the faded start and finish in the preview, turn on {{> courseIncludeRecord}} if you want, and pick how to send it.
  3. The other person taps the link, or scans the QR code with {{> courseScanQr}} in {{> courseReceive}} from the {{> courseShelfTitle}} row or {{> courseTitle}}, then saves it in the {{> courseReceivedTitle}} window.

{{> courseReceive}} also has {{> coursePasteLink}} and {{> courseImportFile}}. The camera turns on only when you tap {{> courseScanQr}}. Opened in a phone browser, the link shows the course drawing and distance with a button to open it in the app. Courses made from a route you ran have the start and finish areas hidden at random and your privacy zones removed, whether you send a link, QR code, or file. To hide more places such as home, use {{> courseHideMore}} in the window. Courses you drew go as drawn. Times and heart rate aren't included. An included record carries only the time for each stretch, with no date or clock time, counted again from the start of the trimmed line. For long courses you'll see {{" courseTooLongForLink}}, so send a file.

Hiding your location

When you send a course made from a route you ran, or export a run as a file, a random length is cut from around the start and finish. Set areas around home or work as {{> runPrivacyZones}}.

  1. Open {{> tabMenu > runSettingsTitle}}.
  2. Under {{> runSettingsTrim}}, pick a distance to hide (200 m by default). A random 0–200 m is added to it before cutting.
  3. Under {{> runPrivacyZones}}, tap {{> runPrivacyZoneAdd}} to hide the area around where your most recent run started.

The center of each hidden circle is moved a little from the real spot and fixed once, so averaging many shared runs doesn't reveal the place. Courses you drew aren't where you actually ran, so they're sent as drawn. Result maps and records on your own device aren't hidden and stay as they are.

Running with a watch

You can measure with Apple Watch, Galaxy Watch, or a Garmin watch. Pick {{> runDeviceWatch}} on the start screen, then start on the watch.

  1. Connect your watch first.
  2. On the start screen, under {{> runDeviceTitle}}, pick {{> runDeviceWatch}} and tap {{> runStart}}.
  3. When your phone shows {{" runSourceWatchWaiting}}, start in FitBoost on the watch.

The watch shows pace ranges, steps, and the course line, and vibrates when you leave the range. Apple Watch also vibrates 5 seconds before the next step. When you finish, the run comes to your phone. Choose satellite settings for Garmin watches in {{> tabMenu > runSettingsTitle > runSettingsSatellite}}; {{> runSettingsSatelliteMax}} uses more battery. The watch home screen follows today's plan on your phone and puts running first on run days; with no plan, it follows your main focus.

Free and paid in running

Measuring is all free. PRO adds analysis that interprets your runs and structured workouts, and PRO+ adds a weekly running plan that works around strength recovery.

  • Free: measuring with your phone or three kinds of watch, live pace and heart rate, auto laps and splits, reference result, pace zones, and predicted times, my courses (same-route detection, drawing, files, receiving), sending by link, QR code, or file, GPX export, importing GPX or TCX runs from other apps, privacy zones, writing to health apps, the single-run limit, metric evidence, and the running coach (today's prescription and reasons, leg recovery, pace zones, this week's distance).
  • PRO: grade-adjusted pace, heart-rate drift, heart-rate load, session load, critical speed, fitness and fatigue combined with strength, structured workouts (step-by-step threshold, interval, and hill sessions), race-day mode (goal pacer and finish prediction), and my segment records on courses.
  • PRO+: weekly hybrid plans, including running-focused ones (with easing off the day after a leg workout), and the readiness check in the {{> sectionRunning}} section.

Locked tiles show a lock, and tapping one opens the FitBoost PRO screen. If your subscription ends, analysis values for runs you've already recorded aren't deleted, only hidden. There are no ads on the running screen.
